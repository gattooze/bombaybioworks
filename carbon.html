Carbon science

The carbon
doesn't go back.

Not all carbon removal is the same. There is a large difference between carbon that stays fixed for five hundred years and carbon that returns to the atmosphere within a decade. We operate only in the first category — and we can prove it.

Why it matters

Why fixed carbon is different.

The atmosphere doesn't care about intentions. It cares about where the carbon actually ends up, and how long it stays there.

01

Biological carbon is short-cycle by default.

Plants absorb CO₂ as they grow. When they decompose or burn, that carbon goes straight back. The cycle is roughly neutral — but only roughly, only when the biomass is left to complete it naturally. When you harvest and burn, you accelerate the return.

The only way to break the cycle is to interrupt decomposition and trap the carbon in a form that resists biological breakdown — for far longer than the plant itself would have survived.

02

Thermal conversion creates a structure that lasts.

When biological material is heated in a low-oxygen environment, its chemical structure reorganises. Volatile compounds leave as gas. What remains is a carbon-rich solid with a highly stable aromatic structure — one that resists microbial decomposition for centuries.

This is not a theoretical claim. The same process, occurring naturally in ancient fires, created carbon deposits still present in soils today — some thousands of years old.

03

Permanence is a material property, not a claim.

We produce carbon in three grades, each with defined permanence characteristics. Soil-grade biochar is the most permanent — fixed for centuries in the ground. Combustion-grade carbon displaces fossil fuel, cutting net atmospheric additions this year. Activated carbon stays in filtration systems for years before reclassification.

All three are measured, graded, and verified. None of them are offsets.

The carbon chain

From atmosphere
to fixed.

Stage 01
Atmospheric CO₂

Carbon absorbed from the air by growing biological material over months or years.

Stage 02
Biological feedstock

Harvested agricultural residue — assessed for quality and processed for conversion.

Stage 03
Thermal conversion

Controlled heat, low oxygen, measured conditions. Biological carbon becomes fixed carbon.

Stage 04
Grading

Output measured against specification. Biochar, activated carbon, or fuel — each to grade.

Stage 05
Verified permanence

Batch record, third-party audit, permanence class assigned. Carbon accounted for.

How we measure

The methodology.

We don't estimate. We measure at intake, measure at output, and have a third party check our arithmetic. Every tonne we claim removed has a paper trail with three authors.

Mass balance accounting

Carbon entering the system — in the feedstock — is weighed against carbon leaving it, in each output stream: biochar, fuel, activated carbon, and process gas. Nothing is assumed or extrapolated. The numbers close or we don't report.

Third-party verification

Independent auditors review our feedstock documentation, conversion records, and output test results. They confirm our mass balance and sign off on carbon removal claims. We report what they confirm — not what we calculated ourselves.

Batch-level ledger

Each production run generates a record linking feedstock origin, conversion parameters, quality test results, and dispatch destination. The record is the basis for every permanence claim we make and every report we issue to customers.

Permanence classes

Not all removal
lasts equally.

We produce carbon in three permanence classes. Soil-grade biochar achieves the longest — centuries in the ground, verified by reference samples from ancient fire events. Combustion-grade carbon delivers this-year impact by displacing fossil fuels in industrial processes. Activated carbon stays fixed in filtration systems until end of service, when it's reclassified and managed.

Each class is tracked separately. No averaging. No bundling.
