01 Biochar

Applied to soil.
Stays for centuries.

Biochar is porous, stable carbon derived from biological material through thermal conversion. When worked into agricultural soil, it does two things at once: it improves the soil — water retention, microbial activity, nutrient cycling — and it stores carbon in a form that resists biological breakdown for hundreds of years.

It is not a fertiliser. It does not add nutrients directly. It creates the conditions in which the soil's own biology can do more with less. Used alongside conventional inputs, it typically reduces requirement and improves yield over time.

From a carbon accounting perspective, biochar is one of the most durable removal pathways available — verified permanence at scale, with no technological lock-in risk.

02 Activated Carbon

What it contacts
comes out cleaner.

Activated carbon is biochar taken one step further. A secondary activation process — controlled exposure to steam or CO₂ at high temperature — dramatically expands the internal pore structure, creating surface areas measured in hundreds of square metres per gram.

That surface area is where adsorption happens. Contaminants, impurities, dissolved organics, heavy metals — they bind to the carbon's internal surfaces as liquid or gas passes through. The output is cleaner than the input.

We produce activated carbon in powder, granular, and pellet form. Surface area, iodine number, and ash content are tested per batch against application requirements.

03 Carbon Fuel

Burns where coal burns.
Costs the atmosphere less.

Dense carbon fuel is what happens when you optimise for energy — high fixed carbon content, low moisture, low ash, consistent calorific value. It fits directly into industrial combustion infrastructure designed for coal: boilers, kilns, furnaces, co-firing systems.

The carbon it releases when burned was atmospheric CO₂ a year ago — absorbed by growing biological material, not sequestered underground for three hundred million years. The net atmospheric addition per unit of energy is a fraction of what coal delivers.

We supply industrial buyers in India and export markets including Japan and South Korea, where biomass carbon fuel qualifies under national low-carbon procurement mandates.
