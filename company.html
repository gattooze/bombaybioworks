Who we are.

A manufacturing company working in fixed carbon. Based in Maharashtra. Focused on getting the product right before we talk about scale.

What we're doing

Carbon products that exist when you're done paying for them.

Voluntary carbon markets built a lot of infrastructure around numbers on spreadsheets. Projects that promised removal but couldn't prove it. Credits that expired, doubled, or just turned out to be wrong.

We make physical carbon products. Biochar that goes into soil. Activated carbon that goes into filters. Dense fuel that displaces coal in industrial boilers. None of these require anyone to take our word for it — the product is the proof.

We're not building an offset business. We're building a carbon materials company. The products are real. The specs are public. The batches are traceable.

The agriculture sector produces surplus residue. We turn it into fixed carbon and sell it. That's the whole model.

India's agricultural sector generates hundreds of millions of tonnes of residue annually — a material most farmers burn to clear fields before the next cycle. We use that residue as feedstock. Our product goes back into the same system, as a soil amendment that improves the land it came from.

Where we operate

Built in India, for a global market in fixed carbon.

We operate out of Maharashtra. The feedstock is local. The conversion infrastructure is local. The quality testing is done by accredited Indian labs before product leaves the facility.

Our customers are mostly outside India — activated carbon going to water treatment operators in Southeast Asia, carbon fuel going to industrial consumers in Japan and South Korea, biochar going to soil restoration projects where the carbon removal credit has commercial value.

India is a good place to build this company. Feedstock is abundant, energy costs are manageable, and the regulatory environment for this category is still being written — which means we can help define what good looks like rather than conforming to someone else's standard.

Why India

The feedstock question has a geography.

Most advanced carbon removal projects are designed in rich countries and executed in poor ones. The infrastructure, the financing, and the off-take go one direction. The labor and land go the other.

We're not describing a clean version of that arrangement. We're a company built where the feedstock is, selling into markets that value what we make, accountable to the same standards we'd hold anyone else to.

01

Feedstock at scale. India produces agricultural residue in quantities that make commercial biochar production viable without competing with food or fiber supply chains.

02

Industrial infrastructure. Maharashtra has an established industrial manufacturing base. We're not asking the grid, the roads, or the workforce to do something new.

03

Export access. Port access to Japan, South Korea, and Southeast Asia — our primary activated carbon and fuel markets — is competitive from the west coast of India.

04

Regulatory moment. India's carbon market regulation is early. Being here now means having a role in setting standards rather than inheriting them.
