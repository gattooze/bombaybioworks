Built for biomass, not adapted from coal

Most activation plants in the world were designed around coal. Ours is built around the chemistry of agricultural residue specifically, which behaves differently at every stage from drying through to activation.

That difference in design is what lets the same feedstock the country already burns become something worth keeping instead.

The process

Four steps, one continuous run

  1. 01FeedstockAgricultural residue arrives, is dried, and graded before it enters the process.
  2. 02PyrolysisHeated in a low oxygen environment. Volatile compounds are driven off, fixed carbon remains.
  3. 03ActivationSteam opens the internal pore structure that gives the carbon its adsorption capacity.
  4. 04GradingSized, tested, and matched to the specification a buyer or a field actually needs.
Why it does not emit

Nothing here is burnt off and lost

The gas driven off during pyrolysis is not vented. It is recirculated back into the process as fuel for the next batch, and the heat generated during activation is recovered rather than exhausted. The result is a closed loop that needs far less external energy than a conventional coal fired kiln.

The same comparison, from a different angle. Producing coal based activated carbon emits 18.28 kilograms of CO2 equivalent per kilogram made. Our route emits 8.60. That gap exists because the feedstock is renewable and the process recovers what a coal plant simply releases.
Chemistry gets you the reaction. Doing it at scale is the craft.

Anyone can carbonise biomass in a lab. Holding a consistent spec across a continuous industrial run, batch after batch, is the actual work.
